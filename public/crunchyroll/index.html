Questions

Where is Crunchyroll available?

Almost everywhere. Not in: China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria.

How does this page check Crunchyroll?

Crunchyroll runs behind Cloudflare, whose /cdn-cgi/trace on Crunchyroll's own domain returns the country that connection comes from, and lets any page read it. That country is compared with Crunchyroll's published list. It is the same edge Crunchyroll itself checks you at.

Crunchyroll says not available, but my proxy is in a supported country?

Each service places your IP with its own database, and they don't always agree: a proxy can be in Japan for one and Hong Kong for another. Some services also turn away addresses of datacenters and known VPNs. The country shown is the one Crunchyroll itself sees.

Why check from the browser?

Rule-based proxies (Clash, Surge, Shadowrocket, sing-box…) pick a route per website. A script on a server only sees the server's own IP; this page asks every service from your browser, so each request takes the route your rules give it.

What do Own API, Own server, Redirects and Inferred mean?

How sure a verdict is, strongest first. Own API: the service's own region API answered. Own server: the service's own Cloudflare edge says which country it sees, compared with its published list. Redirects: the page counted where the service sent you. Inferred: the service can't be read at all; it is reachable, and the country is the one most services see.

Why don't all services see the same country?

Your rules may send them through different proxies, and each service locates IP addresses with its own database. When the countries differ, the page lists which services see which.

Why does the time zone matter?

AI services compare the browser's time zone with the IP's country. A browser in Asia/Shanghai on a US IP is a typical proxy signal and can bring stricter checks.

Is anything sent to this site?

No. Your browser asks each service directly; this site only serves static files and never sees your results. Hide IPs before a screenshot.
